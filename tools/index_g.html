About the project

Linking plant traits, disease stress and Earth Observation

HYDRA-EO targets early detection and attribution of biotic and abiotic stress in key Mediterranean and temperate crops. The project exploits high-resolution hyperspectral and thermal data, radiative transfer models and hybrid machine learning to derive robust indicators of crop health across scales.

By integrating canopy reflectance, sun-induced fluorescence, and thermal emission with radiative transfer modeling, HYDRA-EO retrieves functional plant traits linked to photosynthesis, water status, and structural condition. These traits provide an interpretable bridge between EO observables and crop stress processes.

The project focuses on a representative set of crop–pathogen systems (olive, citrus, pistachio, grapevine), covering fungal and bacterial diseases as well as combined heat and drought stress. Multi-sensor observations are acquired from field plots to satellite footprints, enabling spectral-spatial upscaling.

HYDRA-EO contributes to European food security and plant health monitoring by providing operational building blocks for digital twins of agriculture and early-warning systems for pests and diseases.

Crop diseases & stress

From leaf-level infection to canopy-scale detection

HYDRA-EO investigates how spectral and thermal signatures respond to multiple stressors, including pests, diseases, and combined drought-heat impacts. The project explores how leaf-level processes such as stomatal closure, pigment degradation and canopy temperature changes translate into spatial patterns detectable by EO.

Field campaigns and UAV flights over orchards and vineyards provide detailed observations of infection gradients, structural damage, and physiological decline. These datasets are used to calibrate and validate radiative transfer models and machine learning algorithms, ensuring that disease indicators are both spectrally robust and biologically meaningful.

Methods & Data

Hybrid RTM–ML pipeline for traits and stress retrieval

HYDRA-EO combines physically-based radiative transfer models with data-driven methods to translate multi-sensor spectral information into plant traits and stress indicators, ensuring interpretability and transferability across sensors and sites.

  • Radiative transfer simulations: PROSAIL and SCOPE are used to simulate canopy reflectance, fluorescence and thermal emission under varying structural and physiological conditions.
  • LUTs and emulators: large look-up tables (LUTs) are generated and combined with machine learning emulators to approximate RTM outputs efficiently.
  • Hybrid inversion: regression and classification models (e.g. random forests, Gaussian processes, deep learning) are trained to retrieve plant traits and classify stress regimes.
  • Multi-scale EO integration: field, UAV, airborne and satellite observations (Sentinel-2, PRISMA, EnMAP, FLEX, CHIME) are harmonised through spectral resampling and BRDF-aware workflows.
  • Validation & benchmarking: retrieved traits and stress indicators are validated against in-situ measurements, physiological data and disease assessments.
Key datasets and missions:
  • UAV hyperspectral VNIR–SWIR and thermal imagery over orchards.
  • Airborne campaigns over pilot sites for high-resolution spectral mapping.
  • Satellite imagery from Sentinel-2 MSI, PRISMA, EnMAP, FLEX (FLEX_AO), and CHIME-like simulations.

Work plan

Work packages and project structure

HYDRA-EO is organised in work packages that align user requirements, EO data acquisition, RTM–ML development, validation activities and exploitation of results.

WP1
User needs & system design

Definition of requirements from stakeholders, ESA and end-users. Design of the multi-sensor EO concept, use cases and target crop–pathogen systems.

WP2
Data acquisition & harmonisation

Planning and execution of field, UAV and airborne campaigns. Pre-processing, spectral harmonisation and co-registration of multi-scale EO datasets.

WP3
RTM–ML development

Development of hybrid RTM–ML inversion chains for retrieval of biochemical, structural and thermal traits, and classification of disease and stress regimes.

WP4
Validation & exploitation

Validation of retrievals using in-situ measurements and disease assessments, and preparation of guidelines, tools and demonstration cases for ESA and end-users.

ESA & EU context

HYDRA-EO is part of ESA activities on digital twins, agriculture and plant health. The project develops methods and tools that can be integrated into operational monitoring workflows and future Copernicus-like missions focusing on hyperspectral and thermal observations.

The consortium works in close interaction with ESA technical officers to ensure that project outcomes are aligned with long-term European EO strategies.
