Hybrid Machine Learning & Earth Observation for Multi-Stressor Crop Disease and Pest Detection

HYDRA-EO is an ESA-funded project that combines hyperspectral, thermal and fluorescence sensing with radiative transfer models and hybrid machine learning to detect biotic and abiotic stress in key crops across Spain, Italy and the Netherlands.

Duration: 2026–2028   ·   Prime contractor: Wageningen University (WU) · Action: 1-12684

Hyperspectral Thermal Fluorescence Radiative Transfer Machine Learning

HYDRA-EO develops ESA-oriented workflows and tools that bridge high-resolution field and UAV measurements with satellite missions such as Sentinel-2, PRISMA, EnMAP, FLEX and future CHIME and LSTM.

Scientific objectives

HYDRA-EO aims to retrieve functional plant traits and disentangle overlapping stress signals (drought vs. disease) using a hybrid modelling framework that couples radiative transfer simulations with machine learning across multiple EO platforms and crops.

Functional traits

Retrieve key functional traits

Estimate chlorophyll, anthocyanins, leaf area index, canopy temperature, water status, nitrogen and red-edge indices as well as SIF-related indicators from hyperspectral, multispectral and thermal data using PROSAIL and SCOPE-based simulations combined with machine learning.

Stressors

Disentangle drought and disease

Separate spectral and thermal fingerprints of drought, fungal infections (e.g. Flavescence Dorée) and viral diseases (e.g. Potato virus Y), enabling early detection and improved resilience assessments in agricultural systems.

Scaling

Bridge scales & missions

Quantify how trait signals degrade from UAV and airborne hyperspectral data to satellite missions such as Sentinel-2, PRISMA, EnMAP, FLEX, CHIME and LSTM, and design optimised bands and indices for operational monitoring.

Impact

Operational EO tools for users

Deliver open, ESA-ready tools and Shiny interfaces that translate advanced radiative-transfer–thermal analysis and hybrid machine learning into practical decision support for agronomists, advisors and policy stakeholders.

Crop–stressor scenarios

HYDRA-EO focuses on five strategic crops and a palette of biotic and abiotic stressors, co-designed with European priorities on regulated plant pests and emerging diseases.

Alfalfa (Italy)

Drought and water-management experiments at Italian test sites are used to analyse structural and physiological changes in alfalfa using hyperspectral, thermal and flux data.

Potato (Netherlands)

Multi-stressor trials including Potato virus Y and fungal infections, combining biochemical leaf measurements, UAV imaging and Sentinel-2 / PRISMA / EnMAP observations.

Grapevine (Italy)

Vineyards infected with Flavescence Dorée and other fungal pathogens support the development of spectral–thermal indicators for early disease detection and trait retrieval in perennial crops.

Pistachio & Olive (Spain)

CIAG-IRIAF orchards with inoculated fungal and bacterial pathogens (e.g. Septoria spp., Pseudomonas savastanoi) under varying water regimes, monitored using UAV hyperspectral, thermal and aircraft-borne sensors to build a unique multi-sensor stress library.

Methods & workflows

HYDRA-EO combines radiative transfer modelling, hybrid machine learning and multi-scale EO observations.

Radiative transfer

RTMs & spectral libraries

Leaf and canopy optical models (PROSPECT, PROSAIL, FLUSPECT, fourSAIL, INFORM) and soil RTMs (MARMIT) are integrated through the ToolsRTM package to generate multi-sensor spectral libraries and trait simulations.

Energy balance & SIF

SCOPE-based simulations

SCOPEinR is used to simulate energy balance, canopy temperature and sun-induced fluorescence, linking thermal and SIF signals with photosynthesis and water stress.

Hybrid ML

Physics-informed ML

Hybrid machine-learning approaches combine RTM-based priors with data-driven models (RF, GPR, deep learning) to retrieve traits and classify crop stress, with explicit uncertainty characterisation.

Partners

Wageningen Environmental Research (WENR, NL)

EO data processing, spatial analysis, and operational agro-environmental applications.


CNR-IBE – Institute of BioEconomy (IT)

Italian test sites, vine/alfalfa trials, airborne campaigns and flux measurements.


CIAG-IRIAF – Agro-environmental Research Centre (ES)

Pistachio & olive orchards, inoculation trials, disease scoring and UAV monitoring.
