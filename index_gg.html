HYDRA-EO: Hybrid Machine Learning
for multi-stressor crop disease detection

HYDRA-EO develops a hybrid framework that couples radiative transfer models with machine learning to detect and attribute crop stress from field to satellite scales, using hyperspectral, thermal and multi-sensor EO data.

Project overview

Understanding crop stress across scales

HYDRA-EO links leaf-level physiological processes with canopy-scale Earth Observation signals to support early detection of crop diseases, pests and combined stressors.

The project addresses key Mediterranean and temperate crop systems (olive, citrus, pistachio, grapevine) and develops interpretable indicators of plant health based on hyperspectral, fluorescence and thermal data.

Multi-sensor datasets from field instruments, UAV platforms, airborne campaigns and satellite missions are combined with radiative transfer models to retrieve functional traits related to photosynthesis, structure and water status.

Outputs from HYDRA-EO are designed to be integrated into digital twins of agriculture and operational crop monitoring services.

Diseases & multi-stressor detection

From leaf symptoms to satellite indicators

HYDRA-EO explores how spectral and thermal responses to infection, drought and heat stress can be translated into robust EO-based indicators for different crop–pathogen systems.

Field campaigns capture gradients of infection and stress using detailed visual assessments, physiological measurements and proximal sensing. These observations are used to calibrate and validate models that connect disease processes with hyperspectral and thermal signatures.

The resulting indicators are designed to be transferable between sites and sensors, supporting monitoring of plant health at regional scale.

Methods & data

Hybrid radiative transfer and machine learning pipeline

The HYDRA-EO methods combine physically-based radiative transfer models with data-driven machine learning to derive plant traits and stress indicators from multi-sensor EO data.

  • Simulation of canopy reflectance, fluorescence and thermal emission using PROSAIL and SCOPE.
  • Generation of look-up tables and emulators covering structural and physiological variability.
  • Training of regression and classification models to retrieve traits and map stress regimes.
  • Harmonisation of field, UAV, airborne and satellite data through spectral resampling and co-registration.
  • Validation against in-situ traits, flux measurements and disease assessments.
Key EO datasets:
  • UAV hyperspectral VNIR–SWIR and thermal imagery.
  • Airborne hyperspectral campaigns over pilot sites.
  • Sentinel-2 MSI, PRISMA, EnMAP, FLEX (FLEX_AO) and CHIME-like configurations.

All processing chains are implemented in open-source environments and will be made available through project repositories.

Work packages

Project structure

HYDRA-EO is organised in work packages that cover requirements, data acquisition, RTM–ML development and validation/exploitation.

WP1
User needs & system design

Collection of user and ESA requirements, definition of use cases and overall system architecture.

WP2
Data acquisition & harmonisation

Planning and execution of field, UAV and airborne campaigns and pre-processing of all EO datasets.

WP3
RTM–ML development

Development and testing of hybrid inversion chains for functional traits and stress mapping.

WP4
Validation & exploitation

Validation, demonstration cases and preparation of guidelines and tools for ESA and end-users.

ESA context

The project is part of ESA activities on digital twins, agriculture and plant health monitoring. Results will support future Copernicus-like hyperspectral and thermal missions.

HYDRA-EO works in close interaction with ESA technical officers to align developments with the long-term EO strategy.
